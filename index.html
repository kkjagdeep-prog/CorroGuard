LEARN THE SCIENCE

Explore Types of Corrosion

Discover the different ways metals deteriorate and understand the science behind each process.

🔩

Uniform Corrosion

Corrosion occurs relatively evenly across the exposed metal surface.

Example:

Rust forming across an unprotected iron sheet.

⚡

Galvanic Corrosion

Occurs when two different metals are electrically connected in the presence of an electrolyte.

Example:

Dissimilar metal components connected in a wet environment.

🕳️

Pitting Corrosion

Localized corrosion creates small cavities or pits on a metal surface.

Example:

Pitting on stainless steel exposed to chloride-rich water.

💧

Crevice Corrosion

Develops in narrow gaps where stagnant liquid can accumulate.

Example:

Corrosion beneath washers, joints, and metal fittings.

🏗️

Stress Corrosion Cracking

Cracking can develop through the combined effects of tensile stress and a specific corrosive environment.

Example:

Cracking in stressed metal components exposed to certain chemicals.

ENGINEERING IN THE REAL WORLD

Real-World Corrosion Case Studies

Understand how corrosion affects infrastructure, industries, and engineering structures.

🌉
CIVIL ENGINEERING

Coastal Bridge Corrosion

Bridges near coastal areas are exposed to moisture and airborne chloride salts. These conditions can accelerate corrosion of steel components.

Main cause: Salt exposure and moisture
Possible solution: Protective coatings and suitable material selection
🏭
INDUSTRIAL ENGINEERING

Industrial Pipeline Corrosion

Metal pipelines carrying water or industrial fluids can experience internal and external corrosion depending on their environment.

Main cause: Water, dissolved oxygen, and chemicals
Possible solution: Internal coatings and corrosion monitoring
🚢
MARINE ENGINEERING

Ship Hull Corrosion

Ships operate in seawater containing dissolved salts. Their metal surfaces require protection against marine corrosion.

Main cause: Seawater and dissolved salts
Possible solution: Marine coatings and cathodic protection
🏗️
STRUCTURAL ENGINEERING

Reinforced Concrete Corrosion

Steel reinforcement inside concrete can corrode when protective conditions are lost, including through chloride ingress or carbonation.

Main cause: Chloride ingress and carbonation
Possible solution: Good concrete cover and suitable protective systems

These are representative engineering scenarios, not reports of four specific investigated incidents.
